# PV plant grid following

<!-- veragrid-block-introduction:start -->
**PV plant grid following** represents an energy resource and its interface controls. Storage and photovoltaic models combine source-side energy or power limits with converter commands, so available active power, DC voltage, and reactive-power control must remain mutually consistent.

## Typical use

- Use it to study renewable or storage response to voltage, frequency, and power-reference disturbances.
- Respect energy, current, DC-voltage, and active/reactive capability limits during initialization.
<!-- veragrid-block-introduction:end -->

This model represents a grid-following PV plant in EMT form.

### Purpose

It is the EMT PV plant template used when the photovoltaic resource must synchronize to an existing grid angle and inject controlled current into the network.

### Behavior

- Uses instantaneous three-phase terminal voltages as network inputs.
- Computes the available active power from solar-resource-related quantities.
- Applies MPPT-related logic to determine the active-power command.
- Uses a PLL to estimate the grid angle and frequency.
- Generates d-axis and q-axis current references through outer-loop control.
- Produces phase currents injected into the EMT network through the embedded converter block.

### Characteristics

- EMT averaged grid-following plant model.
- Includes PV availability logic, PLL synchronization, and current-control structure.
- Appropriate for converter-dominated transient studies at the waveform timescale.

## How it works

The plant first determines how much active power is physically available from the PV resource. That command is filtered or limited through MPPT-related logic. A PLL then aligns the controller with the grid voltage. Outer control loops convert active-power and reactive-power objectives into dq current references, and the converter block turns those references into three-phase current injection.

## Characteristic equations

$$
P_{avail} = f(\text{irradiance}, \text{temperature})
$$

$$
\omega_{pll} = K_p e_{pll} + K_i \int e_{pll}\,dt
$$

$$
\frac{d\theta_{pll}}{dt} = \omega_{base}(\omega_{pll}-1)
$$

$$
\{i_A, i_B, i_C\} = T_{dq0 \rightarrow abc}(i_d^{ref}, i_q^{ref}, \theta_{pll})
$$

## How to use it

- Use it when one PV plant must follow the existing network angle and inject controlled current into an EMT network.
- Do not use it as a grid-forming resource; use the grid-forming converter family instead when the source must establish its own voltage angle.
